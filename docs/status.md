## What works today

- **World:** a 240×240 playable map generated deterministically from a seed. It has rolling hills, a flat 40×40 settlement plateau, noise-clustered forests, scattered rocks, one lake, and about 41,000 instanced grass patches (a short meadow carpet) with about 950 flower clusters. A ring of forested mountains surrounds the map and fades into the fog.
- **Settlement:** a Town Hall, a market, a well, six houses, a mill with two 2×3 fields and a lumbermill (workers: a woodcutter, a miller and two farmers; population 9 / 34), connected by dirt paths. Two roads leave toward the map edge.
- **Building style:** the buildings are KayKit Medieval Hexagon Pack models at 4× (slightly larger than units, as in most RTS games): a tall castle as the Town Hall (3.5×, about 14 units high, the village landmark), two house styles (half-timbered cottage and a taller house with steps) that alternate by building id, a roofed well, a windmill whose sails turn while the miller works, a lumbermill (its saw blade turns while the woodcutter prepares wood), a market with stalls, the bakery and the mines. Roofs, banners and trims take the owner's color (blue for the local player; neutral buildings keep the pack's yellow), keeping the roof shading. Every building stands in a trampled dirt yard that reaches about two units past its walls and widens in front of the entrance; placed buildings get theirs when they are placed. Fields and the quarry keep their Builder Pack models (the Hexagon pack has no 2 × 3 field or quarry).
- **Minimap** (bottom left): the whole playable area, north up: ground, hills, water, paths, trees and rocks; the fog (unexplored black, explored dimmed); buildings (ghosts in the fog as last seen), walls and gates as lines, and units as dots, all in their owner's color, enemies only while in sight; and the outline of what the camera shows. **Left click or drag** moves the camera; **right click** sends the selected units there; after **F**, a left click attack-moves there. It redraws 5 times a second (size and rate are constants in `ui/minimap/constants.ts`); a redraw took 0.1 ms of script time (median, 1 ms at most, in headless Chrome), and the ground picture is made once at start (about 100 ms). The controls list above it is now collapsed.
- **Fog of war:** the map starts black except around your village. Your units and buildings reveal what they see (serf and worker 7, swordsman and spearman 10, archer 12, buildings 18, Town Hall 28, towers 22–27, walls 8, all in game-data `buildings/sight.ts`); places you have seen but don't see now stay in a dim grey fog: terrain and buildings as you last saw them (a building destroyed out of sight stays there until you look again), but no units. The fog moves softly as units walk. Enemy units and arrows out of sight aren't drawn, so the skeleton camps are a surprise until scouted. The border mountains outside the map are shown dim. You can only order attacks on what you see: the sword cursor doesn't appear over buildings remembered in the fog. Dev builds have a "Reveal map" switch in the Dev spawn panel.
- **Victory and defeat:** you win when every scripted wave has been sent and no skeleton is left (both camps cleared, the horde beaten), and you lose when your Town Hall falls (if both happen on the same tick, it is a defeat). An end screen shows the result, the time, soldiers trained, enemies killed and buildings lost. "Continue watching" closes it and leaves a small "Victory / Defeat · watching" badge; the game keeps running, but your orders are refused. "New game" reloads the page. In a dev build the Dev spawn panel still works after the end.
- **Walls:** pick Palisade (1 wood per segment, weak, quick) or Stone Wall (2 stone, 3.5× the health: 1,400 against 400, slow to build) in the construction menu and **drag** on the ground. The line snaps to 8 directions; the preview shows each segment green (will be built), amber (the stock runs out before it), red (blocked) or grey (already stands), with the segment count and total cost. Blocked segments are left out; when the stock can't pay for the whole line, as many segments as it can pay for are built, from where the drag started. The tool stays active (Esc or right click ends it; another button during a drag calls the line off), and a drag starting near the end of a wall continues from it, so corners connect with a post. Serfs carry the material over and build segment by segment. Walls block walking, can be attacked and fall one segment at a time into rubble. A warning appears when a line would shut your own units or building sites off from the rest of the map with no gate to leave by.
- **Gates:** select one of your wall segments and press **Make gate** (palisade gate: 3 wood; stone gate: 3 stone, 2 wood; a segment still being built is refunded). Serfs build it; until it is finished it blocks like a wall. A finished gate lets your units through and nobody else, and its doors swing open when your units come close. Enemies have to break it (it is weaker than the wall around it).
- **Breaching:** walls don't make you invincible. Soldiers and skeletons on an attack-move or attack order, or chasing someone they see, who have **no way at all** to where they want to go, pick an enemy segment in the way (a gate rather than a wall, if it isn't much farther), break it and carry on, ring after ring. If there is any way around, they walk it and leave the walls alone. An enemy standing outside the wall is still fought first.
- **Wooden tower** (8 wood, 1 stone): serfs build it like any building. A finished tower shoots by itself at enemy units within 11 units: an arrow from its top every 1.6 s (damage 8 before armor), first at enemies in the middle of an attack, then at the nearest. A range ring shows while placing it and when your tower is selected, and the panel lists damage, rate and range. It is weak by design (600 health): 12–14 spearmen, the anti-building unit, bring it down.
- **Tower upgrades:** select your finished tower and pick an upgrade; the panel shows cost, time and what it does, then a progress bar with **Cancel** (full refund). An upgrade is paid at once and just takes time (no serfs); meanwhile the tower doesn't shoot, can still be attacked (with the old type's health) and has scaffolding around it. Damage taken stays. Wooden → **Stone** (10 stone, 4 wood, 30 s; 1,200 health, damage 12 every 1.5 s, range 12) → one of three, each 35 s:
  - **Crossbow** (6 wood, 6 stone, 4 iron): heavy bolts that ignore armor, 42 every 3.6 s, range 13, 1,200 health; the answer to warriors and armored soldiers;
  - **Magic** (8 stone, 8 gold): 16 every 1.6 s, range 12, 1,400 health, slows the unit hit to half speed for 2.5 s; the sturdy all-rounder;
  - **Fire** (8 stone, 8 wood, 3 iron): every 3.8 s a burst of 6 to every enemy within 1.7 units, which then burns for 3 a second for 3 s; range 11, 1,100 health. Strong against tight crowds, nearly useless against a few armored soldiers. It doesn't harm your own side.
    Crossbow and Fire need iron, so an iron mine first (and its camp cleared). The wooden tower's placement preview is tinted like the tower.
- **Being shot by a tower:** soldiers and skeletons that are idle or attack-moving and get shot go for the tower, even beyond their sight or a guard's leash, and idle comrades close by join them (guards walk home once it falls). They break walls to get there if they must; if there is no getting at it at all, they retreat out of its range. Units on a plain move or an ordered attack keep to their order.
- **Skeleton wave:** eight minutes into the game, a horde of nineteen skeletons (seven warriors, six minions, six crossbowmen) appears on the north road near the map edge and marches on the Town Hall, fighting every unit and building on the way; once there they keep attacking what they see. A countdown shows during the last minute ("Skeletons attack in 0:45"), then a red banner when they set out. The heroes alone nearly always lose to it; with 3 swordsmen and 4 archers (or 8 archers, a closer fight) they beat it.
- **Skeleton camps:** skeletons (KayKit Skeletons pack) guard both ore deposits from the start: on the gold deposit ten warriors (axe and shield; 110 health, 2 armor, 16 damage), eight minions (rusty blade; 50 health, 10 damage) and six crossbowmen (range 8, 13 damage); on the iron deposit six warriors, five minions and four crossbowmen. Against buildings they do much less (per hit: warrior 8, minion 4, crossbowman 2). They stand at their camp, attack anything that comes within sight (11, crossbowmen 12: farther than any hero or archer shoots), chase at most 12 units from their spot and walk back afterwards. Killing one pays a gold bounty to the player who struck the last blow (warrior 2, others 1; clearing the iron camp gives 21, the gold camp 34). The heroes alone nearly always lose against either camp; the iron camp falls to them with the five archers the starting gold pays for (there is no iron before it is taken), the gold camp wants 8 archers or a mixed army. The mines can only be built once the deposit is cleared (builders would be killed). Skeletons idle, shamble, taunt between blows and collapse with their own clips.
- **Buildings in combat:** buildings and construction sites can be attacked and destroyed. Health comes from the footprint (30 per square unit, at least 300: a house about 470, a barracks about 1,260) except the Town Hall (3,000); buildings have 2 armor, spearmen deal ×3 to them and arrows only ×0.5 (2 damage per arrow). A construction site starts with 10% of its health and grows to all of it as it is built. Idle soldiers attack enemy buildings in sight when no enemy unit is near (walls and gates excepted: those are attacked on order or when in the way); with soldiers selected, the cursor turns into a sword over enemy buildings and a right click (or F + click) attacks them. A damaged building shows a wide health bar and its health in the selection panel. A destroyed building leaves rubble (the Hexagon pack's destroyed building, sized to the footprint) that smokes for about 45 s; the ground stays flat, the spot can be built on again, and the worker inside is lost. While enemy units are within 15 units, a production building doesn't send out a replacement worker.
- **Commanding soldiers:** with soldiers selected, the cursor turns into a sword over enemy units and a right click attacks that unit (a red marker); a right click on the ground still moves (moving units don't stop to fight). **F** waits for a click: a point means attack-move (walk there, fighting every enemy on the way), an enemy means attack; Esc or a right click cancels, Shift keeps it for more orders. **X** stops the selected units. The selection panel has Attack-move and Stop buttons too. Units that die leave the selection.
- **Health bars** float over hurt and selected units (green → yellow → red, a light frame for yours, a red one for enemies); hold **Alt** to see them over every unit, or tick "All health bars" in the settings.
- **Unit panel:** one selected unit shows its description, health, attack (damage and interval), range, armor, speed and what it is strong or weak against; a group shows each kind with its count and total health, and what they are doing.
- **Training:** the **barracks** (wood 8, stone 6; 6 × 7) trains swordsmen (food 3, iron 2, gold 1; 20 s) and spearmen (food 2, iron 1, wood 2; 15 s); the **archery range** (wood 8, stone 3; 7 × 7) trains archers (food 2, wood 3, gold 1; 18 s). Both are Hexagon pack models with the player's roof color. Selecting one of your finished ones shows a Train button per unit with its cost and time; a unit is paid when queued (up to 5 per building), trains one after another, and walks out of the door when done. It needs a free place in your housing: otherwise it waits ("Waiting for housing"). Clicking a queued unit cancels it and refunds its cost.
- **Soldiers:** swordsmen are KayKit Knights with sword and shield (cape and tabard in the player color), spearmen are Barbarians with a long code-built spear (waist wrap and bracers in the player color), archers are hooded Rogues with bow and quiver (hood and cape in the player color), all drawn 1.15× the villagers' size. Attacks play in step with the simulation: the sword comes down, the spear thrusts and the bow releases on the tick the blow lands or the arrow leaves; between attacks swordsmen raise their shield and archers keep aiming. Arrows fly in a shallow arc to their target. The dead (villagers too) fall, lie for about two seconds, then sink into the ground. In a dev build the **Dev spawn** panel (top left) adds five swordsmen, spearmen or archers for you (left of the camera focus) or for an enemy (right of it); they fight on their own.
- **Heroes:** you start with the **Commander** and the **Archmage** by the Town Hall (the map's `startingHeroes`; there is no recruiting). They stand out by size, a golden ring and their gear, take no housing, gain experience from their own kills (all of it) and from their side's kills within 10 units (half), and rise to level 5 with more health and damage. Each has three **abilities** unlocking at levels 1, 3 and 5 (Commander: Rally, Shockwave, Last Stand; Archmage: Fireball, Frost Nova, Mass Heal); their buttons are in the selected hero’s panel (with its experience bar and the cooldowns), with the keys **Q, E, R**; a **hero list** under the settings shows every own hero with level, health, experience and a fallen one’s revival (click selects, double click moves the camera there). Aimed ones wait for the mouse: a ring of the ability’s radius follows the pointer inside a faint range ring, red with the reason when it can’t be cast there; left click casts, right click or Esc cancels. Self-cast ones go off at once. In a dev build “+150 experience” unlocks levels. A fallen hero leaves a **grave** (headstone, pennant, golden ring; also on the minimap) that nothing can attack and that shows a little around it. Any own unit within 4 units revives the hero in 30 s while no enemy unit is within 8 (the bar over the grave: gold while it goes on, grey while waiting, red while an enemy is near); the hero returns with half health and all it had learned, for free. With units selected, right-click the grave to send them (soldiers attack-move there; serfs stay by it). Numbers: [phase-6b-plan.md](phase-6b-plan.md).
- **Siege:** the **Siege Workshop** (wood 12, stone 8, iron 2; 8 × 7; a yard with a work gantry, a catapult turret, a rock stack and a weapon rack) trains **battering rams** (wood 10, iron 3; 30 s) and **catapults** (wood 12, iron 4, gold 3; 40 s). A ram hits only buildings, shrugs off arrows and falls to infantry; its log swings back and strikes on the tick of the blow. A catapult shoots rocks 15 units, beyond every tower, but nothing closer than 5; its arm releases on the tick the rock leaves, the rock lobs to the spot aimed at, raises dust and hits every enemy around it, so moving units dodge it. On their own, engines go for towers first, then buildings and walls (soldiers leave walls alone). A catapult with a target drives into range and shoots over walls; a ram breaks through. With only rams selected there is no sword over enemy units. Numbers and rules: [phase-6b-plan.md](phase-6b-plan.md). In a dev build the Dev spawn panel adds 2 rams or catapults and raises a **walled base** (stone walls, a gate, four towers, a house; yours or the enemy's) around the view; it fits whole on open ground such as east-south-east of the village around (80, 48).
- **Combat:** every unit has health; swordsmen, spearmen and archers (game-data, starting numbers) fight with armor, cooldowns, reach and counter bonuses (swordsmen ×1.5 against infantry, spearmen ×3 against buildings, archers ×0.5 against buildings; arrows lose most of their damage on a swordsman's armor). Idle soldiers engage enemies within sight on their own and walk back to their spot afterwards (or when a chase goes beyond 12 units); `attack`, `attack-move` and `stop` orders exist. Archers release arrows that fly (speed 18) and land a few ticks later; an arrow at a target that died meanwhile does nothing. The dead lie for 3 s, then vanish; they free their housing at once. A 100 vs 100 battle costs about 0.3 ms per tick (p99 1.4 ms).
- **Rendering:**
  - sky dome, fog, ACES tone mapping
  - a sun at about 48° whose shadows follow the camera
  - chunked instancing for trees (5,700) and ground cover, and per-model instancing for buildings
  - procedural terrain detail and stylized water, with no texture downloads
- **Camera:** WASD and arrow keys, middle drag or the screen edges (toggle in the settings) to pan, right drag to rotate, mouse wheel to zoom. The focus point stays inside the playable area and on the ground.
- **Building placement:** a construction menu lists the ten buildable types with their costs (two balanced rows once there are more than seven); costs the stock can't cover are red, and the ghost says what is missing ("Not enough gold"). A ghost follows the mouse, snapped to a 1-unit grid, rotating in 90° steps, green or red by the shared placement rules (area, water, slope, buildings, paths, trees, rocks, and for mines the deposit: "Must be built on a gold deposit"). A placed building becomes a construction site, pays its full cost from the stock, and flattens the terrain and clears grass under it.
- **Resources:** a top bar shows the player's stock of wood, stone, iron, gold, food and mana (original icons), starting at 30 / 20 / 0 / 5 / 20 / 0, with a short +/- flash on every change. After a divider come the goods of the production chains, smaller: grain and flour (start at 0; flour is made from 5b.4 on). After another divider it shows the population: serfs and workers against housing (Town Hall 10, each house 4; the starting settlement has 8 / 34), red when full.
- **Gathering:** with serfs selected, the cursor shows an axe over trees and a pickaxe over rocks; a right click sends them to harvest (a group spreads over nearby trees or rocks). Each serf chops (3 s) or quarries (4 s) one unit, carries it to the Town Hall, and goes back; when a tree or rock runs out (trees 3–5 wood, rocks 6–12 stone by size) it moves on to the next one nearby. On the way back they hold the load in both arms (low-poly logs or stones). Felled trees leave a stump (two Hexagon pack styles, sized to the trunk) that sinks into the ground after about three minutes and vanishes under any building placed there, rocks shrink as they're quarried, and the ground they blocked becomes walkable and buildable. Five serfs gather roughly 5–6 wood per minute from a forest edge about 35 units from the Town Hall, and about 5 stone per minute from rocks at a similar distance.
- **Serfs:** five start in front of the Town Hall; more are recruited there (select the Town Hall → "Recruit serf"; each costs 2 food and needs a free place in the housing). The button shows the cost and is disabled with the reason ("Not enough food", "No free housing") when recruiting isn't possible. They are KayKit characters (the Ranger without cape and quiver) with baked skeletal animation: idle, walking (2.0 units/s), hammering while building, each unit out of phase with the others, and the scarf in the player color. Primitive placeholder serfs show while the models load, or if they fail to load. Left click or a box selects them (Shift adds), and a right click sends them to the ground point (spread out) or to a construction site.
- **Cursors and command feedback:** the cursor shows what a click would do: select over own units and buildings, move over the ground and build over own construction sites while serfs are selected. Water and steep slopes count as ground: serfs go to the nearest walkable spot. "Not allowed" shows over other buildings, trees and rocks (until Phase 5), and where nothing walkable is near or outside the playable area; a right click there does nothing. A move shows chevrons converging where the serfs will end up, in the player color (0.6 s); assigning to a site pulses an outline around it with a hammer icon above.
- **Farms:** every finished field gets a farmer (straw hat, ochre tunic). The farmer walks from plot to plot of the 2×3 field and sows each one (2 s, hands in the soil); sprouts appear on the sown plots. Once all six are sown the crop grows for 60 s, rising and turning from green to golden in steps, while the farmer waits at the field's edge. Then it reaps plot by plot with a sickle (2 s), leaving stubble, and carries the grain to the Town Hall as sheaves, three plots' worth per trip (the farmer's carry capacity, set per profession in game-data); after the last plot it sows again. The two starting fields begin ripe, so grain comes in from the first seconds. Selecting a field shows its cycle ("Sowing: 3 / 6 plots", "Growing: ripe in 42 s", "Harvest: 2 / 6 plots") and what the farmer does. Fields are walkable: units cross them instead of walking around. Measured in the simulation: a starting field completes a cycle every 170–182 s, about 2.0–2.1 grain per minute (1.2–1.3 when every sheaf was its own trip).
- **Mill and bakery (food chain):** the mill's miller (white cap) waits until the Town Hall holds grain, takes up to 3 units out of the stock as they set off (so no other worker counts on them), fetches them, grinds them inside the mill (4 s each; the miller is hidden and the sails turn, speeding up and slowing down smoothly) and carries the flour back. A bakery (new in the construction menu: wood 5, stone 4) works the same way: its baker (toque) fetches 2 flour per trip and bakes 2 food from each (6 s per flour) while smoke rises from the oven chimney. Food pays for recruiting serfs. The panels say what the worker does ("Waiting for grain in the Town Hall", "Making food inside"). The flour and food are carried as sacks and bread baskets.
- **Mines and deposits:** the map has two ore deposits about 45 units from the Town Hall: gold to the west-north-west beyond the fields and iron to the south-east. Each shows as a soft tinted ground patch (ochre or rust) with a few dark rocks veined in the ore color, and trees and rocks never grow on it. A gold or iron mine (the Hexagon pack's mine: a rock mound with a timbered entrance and rails; wood 8, stone 4) can only be built with its center on a deposit of its ore. Its miner (helmet with lamp, dark tunic) works like the miller: fetches up to 3 food from the Town Hall (taken from the stock as they set off), mines inside (8 s per ore, 1 food each), and carries the gold or iron ore back. The marker rocks vanish once the mine stands. Deposits are infinite for now. Iron has no use yet (the blacksmith comes with military).
- **Storehouse:** a storage yard (wood 6, stone 3; 7 × 6) assembled from Hexagon props: a tent, a lumber pile, a stone pile, pallets with crates and sacks, barrels, a wheelbarrow and a flag in the owner's color. Once finished it works like the Town Hall: every villager drops goods off at, and builders fetch materials from, the nearest storage (goods still count in the one stock). Build one near the forest, the fields or the mines to cut walking.
- **Workers:** a finished lumbermill or quarry gets a worker (the Rogue character, bare-headed with long hair). It works on its own: walks to the nearest tree or rock within reach of its building (25 / 30 units), harvests it, brings the unit back to the building, prepares it briefly, carries it to the Town Hall and starts again. The starting lumbermill has its woodcutter from the start (about 1 wood per 40 s at the forest edge next to it). A worker needs a free place in the housing too: a finished production building without one waits (its panel says "waiting for housing") and gets its worker as soon as a house is finished. Workers show their profession: the woodcutter wears a rust wool cap and a green tunic, the stonecutter a cloth cap and a grey tunic (the farmer has a straw hat and an ochre tunic; hats and tunics for miller, baker and miner are ready for the next milestones), and their neckerchief and cuffs take the player color. Working units hold a tool: a hammer while building, an axe while chopping, a pickaxe while quarrying (serfs too). Workers can't be selected or ordered; selecting their building shows what the worker is doing. The quarry uses the pack's mine model.
- **Construction sites:** a dirt plate with the Hexagon pack's building stages, switching by thirds of the work: building materials (A), then a stone foundation with a timber frame (B), then walls and part of the roof (C), scaled to the footprint. From the second stage a scaffolding yard (scaffold towers, tables, stone piles) stands around it, and a flag in the owner's color marks the front corner; a progress bar floats above. Fields instead rise from the ground (their plots and crop). The placement ghost is the real model, translucent, with its roof in the player's color and a light green (valid) or red (invalid) tint over a matching ground plate. Idle serfs take sites within 30 units on their own (up to the building's `maxBuilders`: house 3, mill 4...). Builders first carry the materials from the Town Hall, one unit per trip, and the work can't get ahead of what has arrived; each working serf then adds the same amount of work (linear). A house near the Town Hall takes about 45 s with three serfs (30 s of work for one serf, 10 s for three, plus the trips). Selecting a site shows its progress, the delivered materials (e.g. wood 3 / 4, stone 0 / 2) and its builders. Finished sites become normal buildings.
- **Pathfinding:** A* on a 1-unit grid (water, slopes, trees, rocks and buildings block) with smoothed routes; units push each other apart instead of stacking. On the prototype map a path takes about 1–1.5 ms on average and a tick with 300 moving serfs about 0.5 ms (p99 about 7 ms when many paths are planned at once).
- **Shared rules ready for Phase 3:** `createHeightField`, `flattenHeightField`, `entrancePoint`, `createBuildRules().isBuildable` and `createWaterMap().isWater`.
- **Server:** a Colyseus server with a placeholder 2–4 player match room. The client doesn't connect to it yet.
- **Performance with fortifications** (measured in 7.7, 334 wall segments, Apple M1 Pro): simulation ticks stay small (mean 1.1 ms, max 9 ms in a 120 v 120 battle with breaching; a tick has 100 ms); previewing a 64-segment wall costs about 2 ms plus 5 ms for the cut-off check, only when the line changes. In headless Chrome, uncapped, dev build, whole map revealed: median frame 3.5–4 ms with or without the walls (about 100 more draw calls). A wall segment finishing or falling takes about 15 ms across two frames (about 33 ms at worst; 7 ms when nothing changes). 240 attackers shut out by a closed ring: worst tick 6 ms in a simulation benchmark.
- **Performance:** a steady 60 FPS (vsync-capped) on an Apple M1 Pro in Chrome, also with 200 animated serfs walking at once. Uncapped frame times were last measured before Phase 4 (about 2.4 ms at the default view).
- **Repair:** serfs repair any damaged, finished own building, walls, gates and towers included: right-click it with serfs selected (hammer cursor), or select it and press **Repair**, which sends the nearest idle serfs. Each serf restores 5 health a second, up to the building's builder limit. A repair from nearly destroyed costs 25% of the build cost (less damage, less, rounded per resource, so walls and light damage are free; the Town Hall is always free), paid as the work goes; without the resources the serfs wait.

## Known limitations

### Computer players (AI.4)

- **Heroes march with the army as ordinary fighters.** They use no abilities, aren't treated with more care, and a fallen hero isn't revived on purpose (only if its own units happen to stand by the grave). That is AI.5.
- **AI against AI rarely ends.** In 40-minute headless matches of three computer players, none had eliminated another (an idle human was beaten after 23–31 minutes). Making matches decisive is part of AI.6.
- **Difficulty is not yet proven to order them.** Easy, Normal and Hard differ in pace, size and behavior, but no tournament has measured that Hard beats Easy (AI.6).
- **A lost opening hurts for long.** Without an iron mine or gold, an AI that loses its army at its first camp has only bounties to rebuild from. With nothing more to come it attacks again with what it has.
- Fights are simple: no positioning or kiting; the army marches as one group, and there is one expedition at a time. A defense sends everyone at home at the nearest attacker. Serfs don't flee.
- It reckons strength from fixed values per unit and health; counters count only in what Hard trains.
- Long marches keep to a square ring between the edge deposits and the middle: made for Four Corners; another map shape may need other routes.
- It builds towers but never walls or gates, and upgrades towers only as the Defensive kind (to stone).
- It gathers within 70 units of its Town Hall only, and never rebuilds a lost Town Hall.
- Distant sites go up slowly (material is carried one unit per trip), the same as for a human.
- The server (`MatchRoom`) doesn't seat computer players yet.

### Rendering

- **R3F warning:** React Three Fiber 9.8 logs a harmless `THREE.Clock` deprecation warning with three r187. It goes away when R3F updates.
- **Shadow distance:** the shadow frustum is capped at 220×220 units, so distant trees have no shadows when fully zoomed out.
- **Water:** it doesn't receive shadows and has no reflections. The open surface is intentionally calm (depth gradient plus a shoreline foam line), with no waves.
- **Blurry ground marks:** worn ground and lake sand are baked into a 1-unit terrain grid, so they look blurry very close up.
- **Paths:** they follow the terrain without flattening, so on steep slopes they would tilt sideways. Overlapping junctions are simply stacked.
- **Building culling:** each instanced building mesh draws all of its placements together. Large player towns will need chunked culling like the trees have.
- **Ground cover fades with distance:** beyond 100–140 units from the camera it shrinks away, so fully zoomed out the far ground shows only the terrain colors.- **Grass ignores trees:** tufts are not cleared under trees or rocks; this is not visible at RTS distance.
- **Models load without a placeholder:** buildings appear only once their GLB files have loaded.

### Assets

- **Temporary models:** the **battering ram** is built in code from simple shapes (the packs have no ram), as are the wheeled frames under it and under the catapult. The bakery is the Medieval Hexagon Pack's blacksmith (yellow variant: a straw-roofed shed with a domed stone oven), flagged `temporary` in game-data until a bakery model exists. Phase 6 (military) will want it as the blacksmith. Hexagon buildings are scaled 4× (castle 3.5×; Builder Pack 3×); the Hexagon pack's CC0 license is in `assets/models/hexagon/`.

- **Two packs side by side:** fields (farm_plot) and the quarry (mine) are still Builder Pack models, and the small props around houses (round garden trees, rocks) too.
- **Pack style:** the KayKit pack has no standalone village clutter; barrels, crates and fences are baked into the buildings. Its farm plots are blocky crop boxes.
- **Mixed tree styles:** garden trees from the pack are round, while forest trees are our own conifers.
- **Production build size:** about 15 MB (was 19 MB before 5b.6 pruning). Only the seven Builder Pack models still in use ship (farm plot, mine, garden trees, rocks; 288 KB), and every Hexagon file is used. The character folder (about 11 MB) is kept whole on purpose: the Knight, Barbarian, Mage and hooded Rogue and the combat, movement-advanced, simulation and special Rig_Medium libraries are unused today but planned for Phase 6 (military).

### Input

- **Cursor crispness on retina is unverified:** the cursors use `image-set(... 2x)` with 64 px SVGs. Headless Chrome screenshots don't include the OS cursor, so the cursor images, hotspots and sharpness were not seen live; the `data-cursor` states and the computed CSS were checked.

### Siege

- **Engines that engaged on their own walk back** to where they stood once their target falls, like soldiers (and give up beyond the 12-unit leash). To carry a siege through, use attack-move or an attack order.
- **A catapult starts on its own only within 16 units** (center to edge of the building); farther targets need an order.
- **A catapult's splash wears down three wall segments at once** (the one hit and its neighbours), so it opens wide breaches.
- **Paths are planned for point-sized units:** an engine can pass a 1-unit slot between obstacles, overlapping both.
- **No crew, no repair:** engines move by themselves and damaged ones can't be repaired. Each takes a housing place.
- **A new attack order cancels the shot being wound up,** and the 6 s reload stays spent (as for all units; it shows more on a catapult).
- **No impact sounds or damage to the engine's look;** a wreck tips over and sinks.
- **Not balanced by playing yet:** the numbers come from simulated fights.

### Heroes

- **Aimed abilities need the hero in range:** the client doesn’t walk a hero into range for you.
- **With two heroes selected,** the ability buttons and keys speak to the first one only; click a hero in the list to switch.
- **Hit and run still clears the camps:** an Archmage that casts Fireball and runs off again wipes out the iron camp in about 1 minute and the gold camp in about 2 (simulated), because skeletons never regain health. Camp guards healing once they are back home would close this; it is a new rule, not added yet.
- **How hard the map is rests on simulated fights:** a bot that attack-moves and uses the abilities, 49 starts per case (`heroBalance.test.ts` repeats 9). Heroes alone still win now and then (iron camp 5, gold camp 4, wave 8 of 49), and heroes who have levelled up beside an army often win alone (from level 2 on, the iron camp and the wave about half the time or more; the gold camp at level 4 one time in three).
- **In a dev build at 1280 × 720** the Dev spawn panel covers part of the Controls button.
- **Revival ignores enemy towers:** only enemy units hold it up, so a grave under a tower’s fire is revived as long as the helper survives.
- **A grave can’t be selected;** there is no number for the progress, only its bar.
- **Building can cover a grave** (it stays; the hero rises beside the building).
- **Placeholder effects:** every ability shows only a spreading ring; no projectile for Fireball or Volley, nothing over boosted, slowed or stunned units. Casting reuses the attack animation.
- **Heroes don't use abilities on their own,** and monsters have none.
- **The Pathfinder looks much like a serf from the front** (the same Ranger model, bigger, with cape, quiver, crossbow and ring); no map gives it yet.
- **Not balanced by playing:** whether a player can afford these armies in time (above all by the wave at 8:00) hasn't been played through.

### Minimap

- **North up, while the default camera looks north-west,** so the camera outline is turned by 45° at the start.
- **No pings or alerts,** and a right click on an enemy dot is a move to that point, not an attack order (attack-move with F fights on the way).
- **Trees and rocks vanish from it as soon as they run out,** also in the fog.

### Units and pathfinding

- **Quick steps (accepted):** serfs walk at 2.0 units/s at the building-matched scale 0.62 (~1.4 units tall). `Walking_B` covers only ~0.66 units per cycle at this size, so it plays at ~3.2× (≈360 steps/min, feet planted). It reads fine at RTS zoom; close up the legs look hurried. A larger scale (tried 0.99) slows the cadence but makes serfs taller than the houses suggest.
- **Animations switch instantly** (no cross-fade between clips). Tools appear and vanish with the work (no draw or put-away animation). Hammer and pickaxe are built in code (the free pack has neither); the axe is the KayKit one.
- **Team color is small:** only the serf's scarf and the worker's neckerchief and cuffs take the player color.
- **Workers don't look for a better spot:** a production building placed where nothing is in reach keeps its worker waiting (the placement ghost doesn't warn about it yet).
- **Carrying is a combined clip:** the walk's legs with the `Holding_A` arms; the upper body doesn't sway with the steps, and the load is put down instantly (no pick-up or drop animation). Bundles are small at the default zoom.
- **Units don't avoid each other while planning:** separation only pushes overlapping units apart, so crowds in narrow gaps can jostle and a stuck unit gives up after two re-plans.
- **Gatherers often work on the far side of a tree,** hidden by its crown from the default camera angle.
- **Goods are kept through other orders:** a serf moved away while carrying keeps the load until its next gather trip delivers it.
- **Roads give no speed bonus.** Fields are walkable; every other building footprint is an obstacle.
- **Farmers are partly hidden while reaping:** they work from the narrow margin between a plot's fence and its crop, so from the default camera the straw hat and torso show, the sickle hardly. Ripe wheat is drawn at 60% of the model's height for this, on finished fields, construction sites and the placement ghost alike.
- **Mines are far to build:** the deposits lie beyond the 30-unit radius in which idle serfs take construction sites on their own, so a mine site needs serfs sent to it (select, right-click the site), and the 12 materials are carried one per trip over about 45 units (a few minutes).
- **Grain still takes walking:** a farmer makes two trips to storage per harvest (about 2 grain per minute per field near the village, delivering to the Town Hall); a storehouse next to the fields shortens the trips (not measured yet). A load of three sheaves looks the same as one.
- **The game pauses in a hidden tab:** the client tick runs from the render loop (fine for single player; the server will be authoritative).
- **Only buildings and own units can be selected;** there is no double-click "select all of type" yet.
- **Building picking uses footprint boxes** with a rough height, not the model shape.

### Walls

- **Gates can't be closed by hand, undone, or made from a damaged segment.** There is one gate per 2-unit segment; no wider gates.
- **The fog darkens the wall preview** over unexplored ground, so parts of a line can be hard to see there.
- **Drag only:** a click without a drag draws nothing; lines snap to 8 directions.
- **A palisade can't be upgraded to stone in place.**
- **Repair is manual:** serfs repair only when ordered; they don't move on to a damaged neighbour (such as the next wall segment) or pick up repairs on their own. Repair also works while the building is being attacked.
- **The cut-off warning** compares against the largest connected area of the map; it can't tell which side of a wall you meant to be outside.
- **Temporary look:** the palisade posts are a primitive tapered log.
- **Ranged units shoot over walls,** at units and at buildings behind them. A skeleton crossbowman can stay just outside a wall, beyond a tower's reach, and chip at what is inside.
- **A wall that isn't closed only redirects:** attackers walk around its ends.

### Towers

- **Balance comes from simulated fights, not from playing** (`combat/balance.test.ts`): one tower against one army, and a benchmark wave of nine skeletons (the map's wave until it was made harder) against a ring around the Town Hall on the real map, with no other buildings. Against the map's nineteen, two wooden towers no longer hold; two crossbow towers inside a palisade do (Town Hall at about 80 %), two stone towers barely, two fire towers don't. Any one special tower, left alone against the nine-skeleton wave, beats it (fire only barely). With two wooden towers inside a ring, the wave loses 7 (palisade) or 8 (stone) of its 9 and the Town Hall holds; in the open the same towers leave it nearly destroyed. Only one two-tower layout is tested: paths limit where towers fit there, and which segment gets broken changes the result.
- **The last skeleton archers can drag a siege out:** their arrows do 2 damage to a wall (140 s for eight of them to bring down one stone segment, many minutes for one or two). Sally out through a gate to finish them.
- **Magic keeps its target slowed** for as long as it keeps hitting (the slow outlasts the reload). Upgrades can't be undone, and only the wooden tower is built directly.
- **Villagers don't react to being shot,** only units that fight. A group that retreats from an unreachable tower stays out until something else draws it back in.
- **No target choice by the player,** and the tower shows no archer or aiming animation.
- **Temporary looks:** the three special towers share one model (`building_tower_B`), told apart by a body tint, the projectile and a prop on the roof (a crossbow, a plain orb, a cone-shaped flame); their roofs keep the owner's color. The wooden tower is the stone walls' corner-post model, tinted brown. No archers are visible.

### Combat

- **One wave only:** the prototype map has a single wave.
- **The HUD doesn't change after the game ends:** the construction menu, training and other buttons stay as they were, and the simulation refuses the orders. The messages are not worded for it: placement says "Cannot build here", the training panel shows the raw code "gameOver" (the recruit button can do the same when nothing else blocks it), and unit orders just have no effect.
- **Victory and defeat are minimal:** only players with a Town Hall are decided; there is one objective (`clearMonsters`) and nothing for player-against-player wins yet. "New game" is a page reload.
- **Camps don't respawn** and have no loot beyond the bounty. Villagers can be killed but never fight back or flee.
- **Enemy units can't be selected** to inspect their stats (only attacked). Health bars are drawn over everything (no depth test), also through buildings.
- **Bow string doesn't draw:** the bow's string-draw morph target is dropped (instanced props have no per-instance morph weights). No hit reactions (`Hit_A`) and no cross-fades between clips.
- **Attack timing is per soldier, approximate:** each attack clip's hit moment (`attackHitAt`) was set by eye; the playback speed brings it onto the simulation's strike.
- **Workers are targetable inside their building** (they stand at its door), and a killed worker is replaced at once like any worker (free, housing permitting).
- **Units walking back after a fight ignore enemies** until they are back at their spot; units on a plain move never fight (by design: attack-move does).
- **Losses aren't refunded:** a destroyed training building drops its queue (paid units are lost), material carried for a destroyed site is lost, and housing of a destroyed house goes with it (the population can then exceed the capacity until more houses stand). There is no repair yet. Ruins stay for the whole match (cosmetic, they block nothing).
- **No rally point:** trained units stand in front of the door.

### Architecture

- **Multiplayer isn't wired yet:** the client runs the tick locally. The server runs the same tick and validates commands with the same rules, but keeps its game state privately (no state sync, no client connection yet), and its starting settlement is neutral, so nobody can recruit there.
- **No persistence:** placed buildings disappear on reload (by design for now).
- **Builders are the only carriers:** there are no separate carrier jobs, and serfs beyond a site's `maxBuilders` don't help carrying. Delivered material isn't shown as piles on the site.
- **The server's settlement has no stock:** it stays neutral, so players on the server can't place buildings until matches have start positions.
- **Placed buildings get no paths** to their entrances (they do get a worn dirt yard).
- **Cosmetic layers are client-only by design:** props, worn ground and grass live in the client (`decorateWorld`); everything gameplay-relevant comes from `@roe/sim`.
- **Settlement data is hand-authored** for one map. There are no generators or editors yet.
- **Footprints are bounding boxes,** slightly larger than the buildings. Selection and collision will need finer shapes.
- **Tests cover the simulation only:** `@roe/sim` has unit tests for placement rules and commands; rendering is verified with browser screenshots.
